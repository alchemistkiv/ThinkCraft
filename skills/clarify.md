# clarify

## Goal

Clarify an ambiguous, vague, or incomplete request by making assumptions, scope, and terms explicit.

## When to use

- The request could reasonably mean more than one thing.
- The success criteria are not clear.
- Scope boundaries, target users, or expected output are not explicit.
- The risk of solving the wrong problem is high.

## Inputs

- Original request or problem statement
- Parts that appear ambiguous
- Known context
- Existing constraints and goals, if any

## Process

1. Lead with the point: say what seems to be requested, then call out where the ambiguity actually sits.
2. List ambiguous terms, missing inputs, and silent assumptions one by one.
3. Rank uncertainties by decision impact so the conversation stays on the questions that would really change the work.
4. Form only the assumptions required to keep moving; label each one explicitly as an assumption.
5. Split the problem into clarified scope, out-of-scope area, and open-question set.
6. Use natural micro-transitions between sections so the response reads like a teammate tightening the brief, not a formal report.
7. Before offering solutions, restate the clarified problem in one clean paragraph, then list the critical uncertainties that still remain.

## Output shape

- Clarified problem statement
- Explicit assumptions list
- In-scope and out-of-scope points
- Critical uncertainties still awaiting answers

## Guardrails

- Write like a sharp teammate, not a consultant.
- Lead with the answerable part first, then narrow the ambiguity.
- Keep structure light unless the ambiguity is complex enough to justify heavier formatting.
- Avoid obvious restatement, management-speak, and self-important framing.
- Use natural transitions such as `What seems clear`, `What is still open`, and `What I am assuming` instead of stiff report language.
- Do not rewrite the problem in a way that expands scope under the pretext of clarification.
- Do not present an assumption as an established fact or user preference.
- Do not read intent into missing context; if uncertain, mark the interpretation as uncertainty.
- Do not turn the clarify stage into a solution, design, or decision document.
- Do not bury critical uncertainty under unimportant detail; only outcome-changing points should stay at the top.
- Do not hide remaining open questions; state clearly when the basis for a solution is incomplete.

## Handoff

Once the problem is clear, use `brainstorm` to generate options, `analyze` to inspect impacts, or `design` to shape a solution.
